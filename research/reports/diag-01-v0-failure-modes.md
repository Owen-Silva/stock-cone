# diag-01: why v0 fails, by question

> **Not financial advice.** This is a research log from building an educational tool. Backtest numbers are hypothetical, carry survivorship bias, and say nothing certain about the future. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything.

Lane `cone-diag`, 2026-10-03. Report only: no product code changed.
Code: `research/diag_v0.py` (`collect`, `check`, `analyze`). Every table below is in
`research/results/diag_v0_tables.md`, generated by `analyze`.

## Method

- **Data.** Only DEV and VAL as defined in `research/PLAN.md`: TRAIN tickers (md5 of the
  upper-cased ticker, even last hex digit) from `universe.UNIVERSE`, with as-of dates before
  2019-01-01. I went one step stricter than PLAN: a record is kept only if the horizon's END
  bar is also before 2019-01-01, so no 2019+ price is ever read as an outcome. Fully offline:
  every CSV under `data/prices/` is loaded into `Engine(raw=...)`, so nothing is downloaded.
- **v0 records.** For each TRAIN ticker, I took one as-of date every 10 trading days, from bar
  252 (or the first valid bar, if later) up to 2018. Each record reproduces
  `Engine.walk_forward` with the engine's own `_select` and `_paths` (K = 250, de-clustering,
  Gaussian weights, vol scaling, the A + h <= as-of rule). It also keeps the extras the
  diagnosis needs: all 19 quantiles, quantiles from the closest 125 analogs, the analog
  distances, the same-ticker share, the vol ratios, the unscaled analog median, the
  walk-forward base rate and mean, realised forward vol, and the 12 features.
  - `check` confirmed that the records match `Engine.walk_forward` (max |diff| 4.6e-8 on KO,
    9 forecasts; that is CSV rounding).
  - Independently, lane eval-01 (`research/reports/eval-01-baselines.md`) got the same v0
    numbers from its own harness: VAL skill +0.005/+0.013/+0.042 there against
    +0.004/+0.015/+0.046 here.
- **Sample.** 47 TRAIN tickers have usable pre-2019 history (38 in DEV, 47 in VAL), giving
  about 24.1k DEV and 7.0-7.2k VAL records per horizon (h = 5 / 21 / 63). Twenty TRAIN names
  have no usable pre-2019 history, mostly the newer, volatile names, so the "volatile" group
  is under-represented (about 2.9k records).
- **Metrics.** Same as PLAN: scaled pinball over 19 quantiles, divided by trailing-63d
  sigma x sqrt(h), with skill = 1 - model/naive0. Also coverage, PIT and Brier against the
  walk-forward base rate.
- **Caveats on the statistics.** Records overlap in time: the h = 21 and h = 63 windows
  overlap from one 10-day step to the next, and stocks move together on any given date.
  Treat small differences (below about 0.005 in skill or 0.01 in coverage) as noise.

When v0's cone is recentred on zero, its skill is about 0 or slightly negative everywhere.
**All of v0's skill comes from where it puts the centre; the analogs add nothing to the
shape or width.** The median v0 cone is 0.95-1.00x the naive cone's width (Q3 table).
Before 2019, coverage is already close to 80% (0.80 / 0.79 / 0.78). The scoreboard's 76% at
3M comes from 2019+ data and the newer, more volatile names, which this lane may not look at.

## Findings

### Q1. Drift: v0's median is inflated by its own vol scaling

Annualised end-of-horizon log return, all dates:

| split | h | v0 median | unscaled analog median | actual median | actual mean | own walk-forward mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DEV | 5 | 0.154 | 0.104 | 0.084 | 0.085 | 0.105 |
| DEV | 21 | 0.200 | 0.137 | 0.137 | 0.100 | 0.105 |
| DEV | 63 | 0.206 | 0.140 | 0.134 | 0.103 | 0.105 |
| VAL | 5 | 0.190 | 0.142 | 0.177 | 0.111 | 0.123 |
| VAL | 21 | 0.203 | 0.156 | 0.159 | 0.121 | 0.123 |
| VAL | 63 | 0.186 | 0.146 | 0.153 | 0.116 | 0.122 |

- The analogs' own (unscaled) median, about 14%/yr, roughly matches the actual median. The
  overshoot is created by vol scaling. Each analog path is multiplied by sig_now/sig_analog,
  and that multiplies its drift as well as its spread. The median ratio is 1.26 because the
  targets are more volatile than the large-cap pool. By quintile of the vol ratio (h = 21):

| vol ratio (median) | 0.71 | 0.99 | 1.26 | 1.65 | 2.35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0 median, ann. | 0.103 | 0.144 | 0.174 | 0.233 | 0.351 |
| unscaled analog median | 0.146 | 0.144 | 0.136 | 0.140 | 0.140 |
| actual median | 0.148 | 0.166 | 0.131 | 0.151 | 0.086 |

- **By stock type** (h = 21): the bias is concentrated in volatile and growth names.
  - Volatile: v0 median +0.318 against an actual median of -0.001 (amplification 2.15x;
    10% of analogs hit the scaling clip).
  - Growth: +0.255 against +0.169 (1.74x).
  - Mega: +0.201 against +0.222 (1.35x; about right).
  - Other: +0.165 against +0.136.
  - Non-pool targets: +0.236 against +0.130. Pool members: +0.169 against +0.154.
- **By era** (h = 21): the worst overshoot came in 2000-02 (v0 +0.346, actual median +0.054,
  mean -0.072) and in 2008-09 (+0.213 against a mean of -0.022). v0's median never adapts:
  it sat at 0.12-0.35 in every era.
- **No timing information.** The correlation between v0's median and the realised return is
  between -0.045 and +0.054 in every split, group and era.
- In 70% of tickers (33 of 47), v0's median is above the stock's own walk-forward mean.

- The cone is not uniformly too narrow; the DEV-fitted widening needed is only 1.03x (21d)
  and 1.05x (63d).
- It is wrong in two specific places:
  - **Calm stocks** under-cover, with upside misses. Trailing vol is too low when vol is low,
    because vol mean-reverts.
  - **Crashes** (2000-02, 2008-09, volatile names) get downside misses. In stressed markets
    v0 is narrower than naive (0.88-0.92x): the scaling clip and the calmer analogs pull
    the width in.
- Regime by trailing return or drawdown matters little; coverage stays within 0.77-0.82
  in every bucket.

### Q3. Vol scaling: trailing 63d vol is unbiased at 1M and 3M but noisy; misses are vol surprises

Realised forward vol (RMS of daily log returns over the h days) divided by sig63:

| h | p10 | p25 | median | mean | p75 | p90 | share > 1.25x | corr(log) | in80 if rv<sig | in80 if rv>sig | in80 if rv>1.5 sig |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.45 | 0.62 | 0.85 | 0.95 | 1.16 | 1.52 | 0.20 | 0.41 | 0.92 | 0.60 | 0.41 |
| 21 | 0.63 | 0.77 | 0.94 | 1.01 | 1.17 | 1.45 | 0.19 | 0.61 | 0.89 | 0.65 | 0.45 |
| 63 | 0.69 | 0.82 | 0.99 | 1.05 | 1.20 | 1.46 | 0.21 | 0.76 | 0.87 | 0.68 | 0.47 |

- **Misses line up almost entirely with under-forecast vol.**
  - When realised vol exceeds 2x the forecast (2-3% of records), the cone covers only
    27-33%, and the misses are mostly downside (55% below q10 at 21d and 63d).
  - When realised vol is below 0.7x, it covers 94-98%.
- **The analogs do not add vol information.** The cone's own implied sigma predicts realised
  vol no better than sig63: mean absolute error of log(rv/sigma) is 0.43 against 0.44 (5d),
  0.28 against 0.28 (21d) and 0.24 against 0.24 (63d).
- **A simple regression helps only slightly.** I fitted log rv on DEV against log sig63,
  log(s21/s252) and log SPY vol21. VAL R2 for log rv rose from 0.35 to 0.40 at 63d and from
  0.03 to 0.10 at 21d. Pinball changed by +0.001. A real vol model (HAR / EWMA,
  vol-01's lane) is needed for a bigger gain.

### Q4. Tails: slightly heavier on the downside; the cone's 5/95 tails are about right

| h | below q10 | above q90 | below q05 | above q95 | z kurtosis | z skew | share \|z\|>3 (normal 0.0027) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.101 | 0.094 | 0.053 | 0.047 | 6.9 | -0.40 | 0.014 |
| 21 | 0.111 | 0.101 | 0.058 | 0.052 | 5.1 | -0.32 | 0.013 |
| 63 | 0.117 | 0.104 | 0.062 | 0.055 | 4.7 | -0.33 | 0.013 |

- The outcomes standardised by sig63 are fat-tailed (kurtosis 4.7-6.9; |z| > 3 is about
  5x more common than under a normal) and skewed left.
- The analog cone already captures most of that at the 5% and 95% points: misses there are
  5.3-6.2% low and 4.7-5.5% high.
- The PIT histogram is flat apart from the lowest decile at 63d (0.117).
- Misses lean to the downside at 21d and 63d by about 1-1.5 pts, despite the upward-biased
  centre. The low tail is too thin, mainly in crash eras (see Q2).
- Empirical DEV z-quantiles instead of normal ones add +0.001 pinball. Tail shape is not
  where the error is.

### Q5. Analog quality: matches are mediocre, and the closest half is no better

| h | nearest d (median) | median d | K-th d (median) | K-th d (p90) | same-ticker share | distinct tickers | analog age (median yrs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 21 | 0.80 | 1.49 | 1.64 | 2.53 | 0.026 (0.033 pool / 0.018 non-pool) | 36 | 11.1 |

- Distances are in the 12-dimensional standardised space, where typical feature values are
  about ±1. The median analog sits about 1.5 units away.
- About 97% of analogs come from other tickers. Most are large-cap pool rows about 11 years
  older than the as-of date.
- Forecast quality barely changes with match quality. By tercile of the K-th distance, 63d
  skill is +0.032 (near) / +0.006 / +0.005 (far), and coverage is 0.781 / 0.780 / 0.775.
  The "near" edge mostly reflects calm, large-cap setups, which also carry the drift.
- **Closest half (125 analogs) is worse on DEV** (-0.004 / -0.007 / -0.001 against v0's
  +0.001 / -0.000 / +0.005) and on VAL (-0.001 / +0.010 / +0.043 against +0.004 / +0.015 /
  +0.046). Coverage falls by 0.5-0.8 pts. Fewer analogs only add noise. There is no
  similarity signal to sharpen.

### Q6. Baseline fairness: the hindsight base inflates "lost to the guess" by about 2x

Per ticker (tickers with 30 or more dates), mean hit rates:

| h | tickers | v0 hit | hindsight majority | walk-forward majority | v0 beats hindsight (share of tickers) | v0 beats walk-forward | v0 − hindsight | v0 − walk-forward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 45 | 0.515 | 0.548 | 0.541 | 22% | 36% | -3.3 pts | -2.6 pts |
| 21 | 45 | 0.551 | 0.582 | 0.566 | 11% | 47% | -3.1 pts | -1.5 pts |
| 63 | 44 | 0.590 | 0.620 | 0.599 | 7% | 48% | -3.0 pts | -0.9 pts |

- The hindsight base is 0.7 / 1.6 / 2.1 pts higher than a fair walk-forward guess, so it
  overstates v0's loss.
- Under the fair walk-forward base, v0 still loses at every horizon, but by half as much or
  less at 1M and 3M. About 47% of tickers beat the guess, not 7-11%.
- In Brier terms, v0's P(up) is still worse than the walk-forward base rate in every split:
  skill -0.004 / -0.011 / -0.005 on DEV and -0.016 / -0.021 / -0.031 on VAL.
  - On DEV, its average P(up) is too high by 0.2 / 1.1 / 2.9 pts (0.615 against an actual
    0.586 at 63d), which fits the drift inflation in Q1.
  - On VAL, the average is about right (-1.5 / +0.3 / +0.4 pts), so the VAL loss is
    noise in P(up) that carries no information, not a level bias.

### Q7. Directional signal from v0's features: essentially no

Method:
- Logistic regression fitted on DEV, with the walk-forward base-rate logit as an offset and
  ridge penalty 10.
- Each of the 12 features alone, all 12 together, and v0's own p_up (as a log-odds shift).
- Scored on VAL. The SE clusters by as-of date. It does NOT correct for overlap between
  successive dates at h = 21 and 63, so the t-values there are overstated.

| h | best single feature | VAL Brier skill | t | VAL hit (wf-majority hit) | ALL12 VAL Brier skill | v0 p_up VAL Brier skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | mkt_dd (coef −0.05) | +0.002 | −5.1 | 0.559 (0.556) | +0.003 | −0.000 |
| 21 | mkt_dd (−0.06) | +0.002 | −5.9 | 0.594 (0.593) | +0.000 | −0.000 |
| 63 | mkt_dd (−0.06) | +0.003 | −8.7 | 0.651 (0.651) | −0.008 | −0.004 |

- The only consistent effect: a deeper market drawdown from its 52-week high nudges P(up)
  up at every horizon. It is worth 0.2-0.3% of Brier and does not change any hit rate.
  mkt_vol behaves similarly (+0.002 to +0.003).
- Every own-stock feature (momentum z5-z126, MA gaps, drawdown, volreg, volume) has a VAL
  Brier skill between -0.001 and +0.002.
- 14 tests x 3 horizons were run. Fitting all 12 together over-fits at 63d (-0.008).
- **Verdict: no usable directional signal for the cone's median.** At most, a small
  market-drawdown tilt on P(up).

## Fix plan (ranked)

Each simulation below changes only v0's centre or width (no re-search). The numbers come
from the "Fix simulations" tables. PLAN's rule is "wins on VAL and does not lose on DEV".

1. **Stop vol scaling from multiplying drift** (`engine.py` `_paths`, or a research model
   file first).
   - Scale only each analog path's deviation from a centre:
     centre + ratio x (raw - analog mean). Or recentre on the unscaled analog median.
   - Simulated (unscaled analog median as the centre, v0 spread): 21d skill DEV
     -0.000 → +0.003, VAL +0.015 → +0.021. 63d: DEV +0.005 → +0.013, VAL +0.046 → +0.060.
     Coverage is unchanged (0.79).
   - It also removes the +0.2-0.3/yr phantom drift on volatile names. It may reduce v0's
     DEV P(up) bias; its effect on Brier was not simulated.
   - This is the only candidate that beats v0 on BOTH splits at 21d and 63d.
2. **Per-horizon width calibration** (`research/` conformal lane, then `engine.py`).
   - Width multipliers fitted on DEV: 0.98-0.99 (5d), 1.03 (21d), 1.05 (63d). They move VAL
     coverage from 0.785 to 0.795-0.802 (21d) and from 0.782 to 0.804-0.807 (63d) at about
     ±0.001 pinball.
   - Better still, condition the width on the vol regime (fix 3) rather than use one
     multiplier.
3. **Better vol forecast, especially mean reversion of calm vol and shock awareness**
   (`research/volforecast.py`, owned by the vol-01 lane).
   - The low-vol tercile covers 0.76 with upside misses. Crash eras cover 0.74 at 63d with
     17% below q10. Coverage collapses to about 30% whenever realised vol exceeds 2x the
     forecast.
   - My crude three-term regression gains only +0.001 pinball (R2 0.35 → 0.40 at 63d). The
     ceiling is real but needs a proper HAR/EWMA plus long-run-shrink model.
   - Expect +0.005 to +0.01 skill and calm-tercile coverage back toward 0.80. This is a
     guess; not verified here.
4. **Centre choice beyond fix 1** (drift lane): the stock's own walk-forward mean.
   - VAL is the best of all candidates (21d +0.025, 63d +0.069 against v0's +0.015 /
     +0.046). But on DEV it is -0.000 against v0's +0.005 at 63d, so by PLAN's rule it fails
     at 63d.
   - A blend, e.g. half own mean plus half the unscaled analog median, is worth testing.
     Do not use zero drift: it scores 0.75 coverage at 63d on VAL, with 19% above q90.
5. **Drop the direction claims from the scoreboard's "beat the guess", or switch to the
   walk-forward base** (`accuracy.py` / `web/`; the coordinator's call).
   - The hindsight base makes v0 look 2x worse at 1M and 3x worse at 3M than the fair base.
     It still loses either way, so the UI should not claim directional skill.
6. **Don't spend time on K, closest-half or distance tweaks.** The closest half is worse on
   both splits, distance terciles barely differ, and the analogs add nothing to width.
   Analog-search tuning (idea 4) is the lowest-value lane.
7. **Optional, small: a market-drawdown tilt on P(up)** (+0.002 to +0.003 Brier skill,
   with no hit-rate gain). Only worth adding if it is free.

## Not verified

- No 2019+ outcomes or TEST tickers were used, so none of the above has been checked on
  TEST. The scoreboard's 76% at 3M can't be reproduced inside DEV/VAL, where it is 78%.
- Fix 1 was simulated by swapping the centre of v0's already-scaled quantiles. The real
  change (de-meaning each path before scaling) also reshapes the spread slightly and was
  not run through the engine.
- VAL (2012-18) was a steady bull market, so any positive centre scores well there. The
  VAL ranking of centres may not hold in 2019+.
- The t-values at 21d and 63d (Q7) ignore overlap between successive 10-day dates, so they
  are overstated. No multiple-testing correction was applied.
- The volatile group is thin before 2019 (about 2.9k records; 20 of 69 TRAIN names have no
  pre-2019 history), so findings about volatile names rest on few tickers.
- The fix 3 estimate (+0.005 to +0.01) is a judgement, not a measurement.
- The per-ticker record files live outside the repo at
  `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Temp\stock-cone-diag_v0_parts\`. Someone wiped `research/results/` once
  mid-run (2026-10-03 about 23:25), which cost the first collection pass.
